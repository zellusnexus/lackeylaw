Alabama Dog Bite Law

Strict Liability — On the Owner's Property

If a dog bites someone on the owner's property or after chasing them off it, the owner is strictly liable — even if the dog had never shown aggression before.

  • You were lawfully on the property
  • The dog bit or attacked you
  • You suffered damages

The "One Bite" Rule — Off the Owner's Property

If a dog bites on public property or another's property, you must prove the owner knew or should have known the dog was dangerous.

  • The dog bit or attacked you
  • The owner knew the dog was dangerous — prior bites or aggressive behavior
  • You suffered damages

What You Must Prove — By Location

On Owner's Property

  • You were lawfully present — invited, delivering mail, etc.
  • The dog bit or attacked you
  • You suffered documented damages

Off Owner's Property

  • The dog bit or attacked you
  • The owner had knowledge the dog was dangerous
  • You suffered documented damages

Common Dog Bite Injuries

Dog attacks are not just painful in the moment — the physical and psychological consequences can last for years. Children often suffer the most severe injuries because a dog's jaws can reach their head and face at their height. Even a single bite can require multiple surgeries and long-term treatment.

  • Puncture wounds and lacerations

  • Permanent scarring and disfigurement

  • Nerve damage

  • Broken bones — especially in children

  • Infections — rabies, tetanus, MRSA

  • Emotional trauma and PTSD

  • Death in severe cases

What to Do After a Dog Bite

Immediately After the Attack
  • 1 Seek medical attention — dog bites can become infected within hours
  • 2 Identify the dog and its owner
  • 3 Get the owner's contact and insurance information
  • 4 Photograph your injuries immediately
  • 5 Report the bite to Animal Control
  • 6 Get contact information from any witnesses
In the Days & Weeks After
  • 1 Keep all medical records and bills
  • 2 Photograph the healing process as injuries develop
  • 3 Document any psychological effects — fear, nightmares, anxiety
  • 4 Do not accept any settlement offer without consulting an attorney

Questions to Ask a Huntsville Dog Bite Lawyer

After a dog attack, legal questions can feel as urgent as practical concerns. The Lackey Law Firm can review the facts, explain potential next steps, and help you understand the evidence needed to build an injury claim.

What information should I bring to a consultation?

Bring photographs of the injuries and scene, the location and date of the incident, the dog owner’s contact details if known, witness statements, medical records, and any incident reports from animal control or police. You do not need every document before scheduling an initial consultation.

Does a "first bite" mean there is no viable claim?

Not necessarily. The location of the incident, the circumstances of the attack, leash laws, and the specific legal basis of the claim all matter. An attorney will review the factual details rather than relying on a generalized “one bite” assumption.

Will homeowner's insurance cover a dog bite injury?

Coverage depends on the specific policy language, breed restrictions, and incident location. A personal injury lawyer can evaluate applicable liability coverage and manage communications directly with the insurance adjusters.

What if a minor child was the one injured?

Inform the attorney immediately that the victim is a minor. Child injury claims involve distinct legal considerations, including who is authorized to file on the child's behalf, procedural safeguards for settlements, and extended filing timelines.

How soon should I contact an attorney after an attack?

You should consult an attorney as promptly as possible so that critical physical evidence can be preserved, witness testimony documented, and statutory deadlines met. Waiting until medical treatment is complete can risk losing key evidence.
