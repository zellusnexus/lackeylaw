Common Types of Construction Accidents

Falls

  • Falls from scaffolding
  • Falls from ladders
  • Falls through unguarded openings
  • Roof falls
  • Falls from elevated work platforms

Struck-By Accidents

  • Being hit by falling objects
  • Vehicle strikes — forklifts, trucks, equipment
  • Swinging crane loads
  • Flying debris

Caught-In / Between

  • Trench collapses
  • Being crushed between equipment and structures
  • Getting caught in machinery
  • Building collapses

Electrocutions

  • Contact with overhead power lines
  • Exposed or improperly shielded wiring
  • Faulty tools and equipment
  • Lightning strikes on exposed sites

Equipment Accidents

  • Crane collapses
  • Forklift accidents
  • Power tool injuries
  • Heavy machinery rollovers

Exposure Injuries

  • Asbestos exposure
  • Silica dust inhalation
  • Chemical and hazardous material exposure
  • Heat stress and heat stroke

Your Rights After a Construction Accident

Workers' Compensation

Alabama requires most employers to carry workers' comp insurance

  • Medical benefits — all reasonable and necessary treatment
  • Temporary disability — 2/3 of average weekly wage while unable to work
  • Permanent partial disability — for lasting impairments
  • Vocational rehabilitation — if you cannot return to your previous job
Workers' comp is a no-fault system — you do not have to prove negligence to receive benefits.

Third-Party Claims

If someone other than your employer caused the accident, a personal injury lawsuit may also be available

  • General contractors or subcontractors whose negligence caused your injury
  • Equipment manufacturers — if defective tools or machinery were involved
  • Property owners — if dangerous site conditions caused the accident
  • Drivers — if you were struck by a vehicle on the job
Why Third-Party Claims Matter

Workers' comp covers medical bills and a portion of lost wages — but it does not compensate you for pain and suffering, emotional distress, or the full value of your lost earning capacity. Third-party personal injury claims often recover significantly more than workers' comp alone. Many injured workers qualify for both.

OSHA Safety Violations

Why OSHA Matters to Your Case

Safety violations are evidence of negligence.

When OSHA standards are violated and that violation contributes to an accident, it strengthens your case significantly. We investigate every construction site accident for OSHA violations and use them to build the strongest possible claim on your behalf.

Common OSHA violations that lead to construction accidents include:

  • Lack of fall protection systems

  • Inadequate or unsecured scaffolding

  • Failure to provide hard hats and PPE

  • Unsafe trenching and excavation practices

  • Unguarded electrical hazards

  • Lack of machine guarding

  • Inadequate worker training and safety briefings

What to Do After a Construction Accident

  • 1

    Report the Injury Immediately

    Notify your supervisor on-site. Failure to report promptly can complicate your workers' compensation claim.

  • 2

    Seek Medical Attention

    Your health comes first. Get treated right away — even if the injury seems minor. Some serious injuries are not immediately apparent.

  • 3

    Document Everything

    Photograph the accident scene, your injuries, and any equipment or conditions involved before they're altered or repaired.

  • 4

    Get Witness Information

    Collect names and contact information from co-workers or anyone else who saw the accident happen.

  • 5

    Preserve Evidence

    Do not allow the equipment, tools, or site conditions involved in your accident to be repaired or disposed of before they can be examined.

  • 6

    Contact an Attorney Before Signing Anything

    Do not give recorded statements or sign settlement agreements without speaking to a lawyer first. Early decisions can significantly affect your recovery.
