Two Rules Every Alabama Injury Victim Should Know

Most Alabama injury lawsuits must be filed within two years of the injury (Ala. Code § 6-2-38(l)). Miss it, and you will likely lose your right to recover anything.
The two-year filing deadline

Two years goes quickly while you are in pain, missing work, and juggling bills. Evidence disappears too: skid marks fade, surveillance video gets recorded over, and witnesses forget or move away. The sooner you call, the more we can protect.

Some claims follow different timelines, including claims against a city, county, or other government body, and workers' compensation claims. Wrongful death claims generally must also be filed within two years of the date of death (Ala. Code § 6-5-410). Confirm your deadline with an attorney as soon as you can.

Contributory negligence: why fault matters so much in Alabama

Alabama follows a strict rule called contributory negligence. If you are found even slightly at fault for your own accident, you may be barred from recovering compensation. Most states are far more forgiving.

Insurance adjusters know this and use it. A casual remark about driving "a little fast" or looking away for a second can be turned against you. Limited exceptions exist, and every case turns on its facts.

Never guess about fault. If you worry you may share some blame, call us before giving any statement and we will tell you honestly where you stand.

Dealing with the insurance company

An adjuster may sound friendly, but their job is to limit what the company pays, often by arguing you were at fault or are not as hurt as you say.

  • You do not have to give the other driver's insurer a recorded statement.
  • Do not sign anything or accept a settlement before an attorney reviews it.
  • We handle the communication and give you straight advice on any offer.

What Compensation Can a Huntsville Personal Injury Attorney Help You Recover?

A claim is about more than proving fault. It is about recovering the money you need to move forward. Depending on your case, damages may include:

Medical Expenses

Emergency treatment, hospital stays, surgery, prescriptions, and therapy, including care you will still need.

Lost Wages

Income missed while unable to work: pay, bonuses, commissions, and sick or vacation time you had to use.

Future Earning Capacity

When an injury limits how you earn, you may recover income lost across the rest of your working life.

Pain and Suffering

Physical pain, emotional distress, and reduced quality of life after a serious injury.

Permanent Disability

Long-term care, home or vehicle modifications, and the lifelong impact on daily living.

Property Damage

Repair or replacement of your vehicle and belongings, plus a rental while yours is in the shop.

Even if health insurance paid your bills and your car is repaired, you may still have a claim for lost wages, pain, and other damages. And if the at-fault driver was uninsured or underinsured, you may have other coverage to turn to.

What to Do After an Accident in Huntsville

What you do in the first hours and days can shape your claim.

  1. Get medical careCall 911 if anyone is hurt. Concussions and internal injuries can show up later, so get checked even if you feel fine.
  2. Report the accidentHave police come to the scene and ask for the report number. You can request your crash report through ALEA.
  3. Document everythingPhotograph vehicles, the road, the weather, and your injuries. Get names, numbers, and insurance details from drivers and witnesses.
  4. Don't apologize or guess at faultStick to the facts you know.
  5. Be careful with adjustersYou don't have to give a recorded statement. Talk to a lawyer first.
  6. Keep your recordsSave medical bills, pay stubs, repair estimates, and notes on how the injuries affect your day.
  7. Call usWe will handle the calls, paperwork, and pressure so you can focus on healing.

How Much Is My Case Worth, and How Long Will It Take?

No honest lawyer can promise a number on day one. Every case turns on its own facts.

What affects the value of a case
  • Severity of your injuries and the length of recovery
  • Medical costs, now and in the future
  • Lost income and any loss of earning power
  • Impact on your daily life, including pain and lost enjoyment of life
  • Who was at fault, which is critical under Alabama's contributory negligence rule
  • Available insurance, since policy limits affect what is realistically collectible
How long a case takes

There is no set timeline. Some claims settle in months. Others take longer, especially when injuries are serious, fault is disputed, or the case goes to trial.

We generally advise reaching a clear point in your medical recovery before settling, because settling too early can leave you paying for care the settlement never covered. We will keep you updated and never pressure you into a deal that doesn't fit what you have been through.

Does a "minor" accident still count?

Possibly. Neck and back problems from a seemingly minor wreck can surface years later, and by then it may be too late to file. A free consultation can tell you whether you have a claim worth pursuing.
