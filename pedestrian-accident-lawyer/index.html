Common Causes of Pedestrian Accidents

Driver Negligence

  • Distracted driving — texting, phone calls, GPS use
  • Failure to yield at crosswalks
  • Speeding — reduces reaction time and increases injury severity
  • Running red lights and stop signs
  • Drunk or impaired driving
  • Failure to check blind spots when backing up or turning
  • Poor visibility — not using headlights at dusk or dawn

Dangerous Road Design

  • Lack of crosswalks in high-traffic areas
  • Inadequate lighting along pedestrian routes
  • Poorly timed or missing pedestrian signals
  • Construction zones without proper pedestrian detours

Pedestrian Negligence

While drivers are usually at fault, pedestrians can contribute to accidents by:

  • Jaywalking or crossing outside crosswalks
  • Walking while intoxicated
  • Walking while distracted by a phone
  • Wearing dark clothing at night
Alabama's Contributory Negligence Rule: If you're found even 1% at fault, you cannot recover damages. This makes legal representation essential — insurance companies will look for any reason to blame you.

Alabama Pedestrian Laws

Pedestrian Rights

  • Drivers must yield to pedestrians in crosswalks
  • Drivers must exercise due care to avoid colliding with pedestrians
  • At intersections without signals, pedestrians have the right of way when crossing

Pedestrian Responsibilities

  • Obey traffic signals — Walk and Don't Walk
  • Use crosswalks when available
  • Walk on sidewalks when available; if not, walk facing traffic
  • Do not suddenly leave the curb and walk into the path of a vehicle
Alabama's Pure Contributory Negligence Rule

Alabama is one of only four states that still uses pure contributory negligence. If a driver's insurance company can show you were even 1% responsible for the accident — whether by jaywalking, walking distracted, or wearing dark clothing — you may be barred from recovering any damages at all. An experienced attorney is not optional in Alabama pedestrian cases. It's critical.

Common Pedestrian Accident Injuries

Pedestrians have no protection against vehicles. There are no airbags, no crumple zones, and no seatbelts. When a vehicle strikes a person on foot, the human body absorbs the full force of the impact. Injuries are typically severe, life-altering, and expensive to treat.

  • Traumatic brain injuries

  • Spinal cord injuries and paralysis

  • Broken hips and pelvis

  • Internal organ damage

  • Broken bones

  • Severe road rash and abrasions

  • Amputations

  • Wrongful death

Compensation for Pedestrian Accident Victims

Medical Expenses

Emergency care, surgery, hospitalization, rehabilitation — often extensive in pedestrian cases.

Lost Wages

Income lost during recovery and reduced future earning capacity.

Pain & Suffering

Physical pain and emotional distress caused by the accident and recovery process.

Emotional Distress

Anxiety, PTSD, and psychological harm resulting from the trauma of being struck.

Loss of Enjoyment

Inability to participate in activities, hobbies, and the quality of life you had before.
