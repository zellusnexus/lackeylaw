Common Causes of Bicycle Accidents

Driver Negligence

  • Distracted driving — texting, eating, adjusting GPS
  • Failure to yield at intersections, driveways, and bike lanes
  • Right-hook turns into the path of a cyclist
  • Left-turn accidents into oncoming cyclists
  • Dooring — opening car doors into bike lanes
  • Passing too closely — violating Alabama's 3-foot law
  • Drunk driving
  • Speeding — reduces reaction time and increases impact

Road Hazards

  • Potholes and cracked pavement
  • Debris in bike lanes
  • Poorly designed intersections
  • Inadequate signage
  • Construction zones without proper warnings

Defective Equipment

  • Faulty brakes
  • Defective tires
  • Broken chains
  • Malfunctioning helmets — failure to protect as designed

Alabama Bicycle Laws

Cyclists' Rights

  • Bicyclists have the same rights and responsibilities as motor vehicle drivers
  • Cyclists may use full lanes when necessary for safety
  • Motorists must give at least 3 feet of clearance when passing

Cyclists' Responsibilities

  • Ride as far right as practicable (with exceptions)
  • Use hand signals for turns and stops
  • Obey all traffic signals and signs
  • Use lights and reflectors when riding at night
  • Children under 16 must wear helmets (state law)
Alabama's Contributory Negligence Rule

Alabama is one of only a few states with pure contributory negligence. If a cyclist is found even 1% at fault for an accident, they cannot recover any damages. Insurance companies routinely try to blame cyclists — claiming they were riding too fast, weren't visible enough, or violated traffic laws. We fight these unfair accusations aggressively.

Frequently Asked Questions About Alabama Bicycle Accidents

What is the deadline to file a bicycle accident lawsuit in Alabama?

In Alabama, the statute of limitations for personal injury and wrongful death claims resulting from a bicycle accident is strictly two years from the date of the incident. Failing to initiate legal proceedings within this two-year window typically results in the permanent loss of your right to seek financial recovery.

Legal Reference: Alabama Code § 6-2-38 - Limitations of Actions (Two Years)

How does Alabama's 3-Foot Passing Law protect cyclists?

Under Alabama law, motorists overtaking a bicycle proceeding in the same direction must maintain a minimum safe clearance of at least 3 feet between the motor vehicle and the bicycle until safely past. Drivers who fail to yield or pass too closely can be held negligent if a collision or forced off-road crash occurs.

Legal Reference: Alabama Code § 32-5A-281 - Safe Passing Distance

Can I recover compensation if I was partially at fault for the bicycle crash?

Alabama adheres to the strict rule of pure contributory negligence. Under this doctrine, if an injured cyclist is found to be even 1% at fault for an accident, they may be barred from recovering any financial compensation from the other party. Because insurance adjusters frequently use this rule to deny legitimate claims, having experienced local legal representation is critical to protecting your rights.

What damages can be recovered in an Alabama bicycle accident claim?

Injured cyclists may seek compensatory damages for past and future medical bills, lost income, reduced earning capacity, property damage (such as bicycle replacement), and physical pain and emotional distress. In cases involving a fatal collision, claims brought under the Alabama Wrongful Death Act are limited by law to punitive damages, which are intended to punish gross negligence and deter future harm.

Legal Reference: Alabama Code § 6-5-410 - Wrongful Act, Omission, or Negligence Causing Death

Common Bicycle Accident Injuries

When a vehicle strikes a cyclist, the human body absorbs the full force of the collision. Injuries are often severe, life-altering, or fatal — even when the cyclist was wearing proper safety gear.

  • Traumatic brain injuries — even with helmets

  • Spinal cord injuries and paralysis

  • Broken bones — collarbones, wrists, arms, legs

  • Road rash and severe abrasions

  • Internal organ damage

  • Dental and facial injuries

  • Amputations

What to Do After a Bicycle Accident

At the Scene
  • Call 911 — even if injuries seem minor
  • Get the driver's license, insurance, and registration
  • Collect contact information from witnesses
  • Photograph the scene, your bike, the vehicle, and your injuries
  • Do not admit fault or apologize
  • Seek medical attention immediately
In the Days After
  • Preserve your damaged bike and gear — they are evidence
  • Document your injuries with photos as they develop
  • Keep a daily pain and recovery journal
  • Do not give recorded statements to insurance companies
  • Contact a bicycle accident attorney before accepting any offer
