Defective Medical Devices

Types of Defects That Create Liability

Type 01

Design Defect

The product's design is inherently dangerous — even if every unit was manufactured exactly as intended.

Example: A hip implant designed with materials that shed metal particles into the bloodstream, causing metallosis.
Type 02

Manufacturing Defect

The product's design is sound, but something went wrong during production — affecting one unit or an entire batch.

Example: A batch of pacemakers produced with faulty battery connections that fail prematurely.
Type 03

Marketing Defect

The manufacturer knew of serious risks but failed to adequately warn patients or prescribing physicians.

Example: Not disclosing the known risk of metallosis when marketing metal-on-metal hip implants to surgeons.

Who Can Be Held Liable?

Liability in defective device cases can extend beyond the device manufacturer. Multiple parties in the chain of distribution may bear responsibility for your injuries.

Device Manufacturers

The primary defendant — responsible for design, manufacturing, and marketing of the device.

Component Manufacturers

Suppliers of defective parts or materials used in the device's construction.

Distributors & Sellers

Hospitals, clinics, and medical supply companies that distributed the defective device.

Physicians

Doctors who failed to warn patients of known risks or implanted a device despite known contraindications.

Compensation in Defective Medical Device Cases

Medical Expenses

Revision surgeries, device removal, ongoing treatment, and future medical needs.

Lost Wages

Income lost during recovery and reduced earning capacity going forward.

Pain & Suffering

Physical pain and emotional distress caused by the device failure and its consequences.

Emotional Distress

Anxiety, depression, and psychological harm resulting from the injury and treatment.

Loss of Enjoyment

Inability to participate in activities and quality of life diminished by the device failure.

Punitive Damages

Available against manufacturers for reckless conduct — withholding safety data, concealing known risks.
